Abstract

Video-Large Multimodal Models (Video-LMMs) lag behind their image and language counterparts due to high computational demands and a complex design space. In this work, we introduce Apollo, a family of state-of-the-art video-LMMs that achieve superior performance while being computationally efficient. We systematically explore the design space of video-LMMs, identifying key strategies in video sampling, token resampling, and integration. We also propose the concept of Scaling Consistency, demonstrating that design decisions validated on smaller models reliably transfer to larger ones. Additionally, we present ApolloBench, a cost-efficient evaluation suite that accelerates research. Apollo models outperform existing larger models across multiple benchmarks, setting a new standard in video understanding.

💡 Introduction

Video understanding in Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) presents significant challenges due to high computational costs and a vast, complex design space. Unlike image and language models, video-LMMs require efficient strategies for sampling and encoding temporal data. Existing methods often extend image-based models to video without fully understanding the impact of design choices on performance.

In our work, we introduce Apollo, a family of video-centric LMMs that excel in performance while maintaining computational efficiency. We conduct a systematic exploration of the video-LMM design space, focusing on critical aspects such as video sampling methods, encoder selection, token resampling, and integration strategies. Our research identifies effective practices that significantly enhance model performance.

We also propose the concept of Scaling Consistency, which demonstrates that design decisions made on moderately sized models (2–4 billion parameters) and datasets reliably transfer to larger models. This insight allows for more efficient research and development by reducing computational overhead.

🔑 Key Findings

  • Finding 1: Scaling Consistency enables reliable design decisions on smaller models and datasets, transferring effectively to larger models.
  • Finding 2: FPS-based video sampling significantly outperforms uniform sampling in both training and inference.
  • Finding 3: Optimal performance is achieved with 8–32 tokens per frame, balancing frame rate and token count.
  • Finding 4: The SigLIP-SO400M image encoder outperforms video encoders in single-encoder setups for video-LMMs.
  • Finding 5: Combining SigLIP-SO400M with a video encoder like InternVideo2 yields the best overall performance.
  • Finding 6: The Perceiver Resampler is the most effective method for token resampling.
  • Finding 7: Incorporating contextual tokens (e.g., timestamps) between video clips enhances token integration efficiency.
  • Finding 8: Multi-stage training schedules with progressive unfreezing lead to superior performance.
  • Finding 9: Fine-tuning video encoders exclusively on video data improves temporal reasoning tasks.
  • Finding 10: Including 10–14% text data in training maintains language comprehension while optimizing video understanding.

📊 Figures and Visualizations

🚀 Apollo Model Family

The Apollo models are built upon the insights gained from our systematic exploration. They employ a dual-encoder architecture, combining the strengths of both image and video encoders. The Perceiver Resampler is used for efficient token resampling, and contextual tokens like timestamps enhance token integration.

We trained Apollo models using a three-stage training schedule, progressively unfreezing components to optimize performance. The training data is carefully composed, including 10–14% text data and a video-heavy balance of the remaining modalities.

Despite their moderate sizes (1.5B, 3B, and 7B parameters), Apollo models outperform larger existing models across multiple benchmarks, demonstrating the effectiveness of our design and training strategies.

⚖️ Performance Evaluation

Apollo models were evaluated across several benchmarks, including TempCompass, MLVU, Video-MME, LongVideoBench, and our own ApolloBench. The results demonstrate that Apollo models set new standards in video understanding tasks.

🧪 ApolloBench

We introduce ApolloBench, a cost-efficient evaluation suite designed to test video perception in LMMs effectively. ApolloBench focuses on questions that require video understanding across five domains: Temporal OCR, Egocentric, Spatial, Perception, and Reasoning. It provides a 41× faster evaluation process while maintaining strong correlation with existing benchmarks.

🔚 Conclusion

Our exploration into video understanding in LMMs has led to significant advancements in model design and training strategies. The Apollo models set new benchmarks in performance while remaining computationally efficient. The concept of Scaling Consistency provides a framework for future research, enabling effective design decisions without prohibitive computational costs. We believe these contributions will pave the way for more robust and accessible video-LMMs in the research community.
